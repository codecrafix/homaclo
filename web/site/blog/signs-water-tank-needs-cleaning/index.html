7 Signs Your Water Tank Needs Cleaning Right Now

By Homaclo · 31 October 2026 · 4 min read

You cannot see inside a rooftop tank every day, but your tap water usually gives clues. If you notice any of the signs below, it is time to get your tank cleaned.

The 7 warning signs

1. A musty or foul smell

Fresh water has little to no smell. A stale, earthy or rotten odour from the tap often points to sludge or algae in the tank.

2. Cloudy, yellow or brown water

Discolouration can come from sediment stirred up from the bottom of the tank or from old, rusty pipes. Either way, it needs a look.

3. Particles or sediment in a glass

Fill a clear glass and let it settle. Fine grit or flakes at the bottom mean the tank has built-up sludge.

4. Green slime or algae on the walls

If you can see the inside of your tank and the walls look slimy or green, algae is growing. This is common in tanks that get sunlight or stay damp.

5. An unusual taste

A flat, metallic or bitter taste in drinking water is a reason to check the tank and the sump.

6. Insects, larvae or a damaged lid

Mosquito larvae, dead insects or a cracked, loose lid mean outside material is getting in. Cleaning is needed and the lid should be fixed.

7. It has been six months or more

Even if the water looks fine, a tank that has not been cleaned for six months or longer is due. See our tank cleaning schedule.

What to do next

  1. Avoid drinking or cooking with water that smells or looks discoloured until the tank is cleaned.
  2. Book a professional tank cleaning. Clean the underground sump as well if you have one.
  3. Check that the tank lid is intact and fits tightly.
  4. Ask for a TDS reading after cleaning so you can see the difference. It is a quick check, not a full lab test.

Health note: if anyone at home feels unwell after drinking tap water, speak to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

Is cloudy tank water safe to drink?

Avoid drinking or cooking with water that smells or looks discoloured. Get the tank cleaned and use the water for drinking only once it runs clear. If anyone feels unwell, speak to a doctor.

Can I just add a disinfectant instead of cleaning the tank?

Disinfectants do not remove sludge and sediment. Physically cleaning the tank is the first step. Follow a professional’s advice on any disinfection.
