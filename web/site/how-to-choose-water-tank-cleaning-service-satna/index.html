Why the choice matters

The water in your tank is what you drink, cook with and bathe in. A rushed job that leaves sludge in the corners, or cleans the rooftop tank but not the sump, gives you clean-looking water for a few days and the same problem again soon. Asking a few questions up front saves money and repeat visits.

10 questions to ask before you book

  1. What is the exact price for my tank size? A good answer is a fixed rate by litres, not “we will see when we come”. Be careful of a low phone quote that grows on the day.
  2. Is the sump (underground tank) included? Ask for both the rooftop tank and the sump, and whether each is priced separately.
  3. How is the sludge removed? A vacuum for the bottom sludge is better than just scooping or flushing it out.
  4. How are the walls cleaned? Look for scrubbing followed by a high-pressure wash, not a quick rinse.
  5. Are the inlet and outlet pipes flushed? Otherwise dirt left in the pipes goes straight back into the clean tank.
  6. Is there any check after cleaning? For example, a TDS meter reading. TDS shows dissolved solids, so it is a quick indicator and not a full lab test.
  7. Can I choose the date and time? A fixed time slot is easier to plan around than “sometime in the day”.
  8. How long will it take, and will the water be off? The tank has to be emptied, so ask how long supply will be affected.
  9. How do I pay, and do I get proof? Online payment with a confirmation is easier to track than cash with no record.
  10. How often do you recommend cleaning? A sensible answer is about every 6 months, sooner for dusty or open tanks.

Warning signs when comparing services

  • No price until the team reaches your home.
  • Only the rooftop tank is mentioned, the sump is ignored.
  • No clear description of how the cleaning is done.
  • Cash only, with no booking confirmation.
  • A promise that you never need to clean again. Tanks collect dust and sediment over time, so regular cleaning is normal.

When should you clean?

Most homes need a clean about every 6 months, every 3 to 4 months if the tank is open or there is construction nearby, and straight away if the water smells, looks cloudy or tastes odd. See how often to clean your water tank and signs your tank needs cleaning.
