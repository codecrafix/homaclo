Solar Not Working or Low Output? An Inverter Error Checklist

By Homaclo · 2 November 2026 · 6 min read

If your solar system is giving low output, showing an inverter error or not working at all, start with a few safe checks: weather, dirty panels, shade, grid supply and breakers. If the problem stays, or you see burning, sparking or a hot panel, stop and call a technician. Never open the inverter or handle the wiring yourself.

Safety first: what never to do

  • Do not open the inverter or the panel junction boxes.
  • Do not touch wiring or connectors. Solar panels produce DC electricity whenever light falls on them, even when the inverter is off.
  • Do not walk on the panels or climb up in the rain or on a wet roof.
  • Do not ignore burning smells, sparks or melted plastic. Switch off the AC supply to the inverter at the breaker if you can do it safely, stay away from the panels and call a technician.

Step 1: Is it really a fault? Check the simple things

  1. Weather. Cloud, haze, fog and heavy dust in the air all reduce output. Compare today’s generation with a day of similar weather, not with a clear summer day.
  2. Time of day. Output rises towards midday and falls in the evening. Low numbers at 9 am or 5 pm are normal.
  3. Season. Winter sun is lower and days are shorter, so expect less than in summer.
  4. Dirty panels. Look at the panels from a safe place. A layer of dust, dried mud or bird droppings lowers output. See solar panel cleaning cost and frequency.
  5. New shade. A growing tree, a new building, a water tank or even a dish antenna can shade part of a panel at some hours. Even a small shadow on one panel can drag down a whole string.

Step 2: Read what the inverter is telling you

Look at the inverter display or indicator lights and write down exactly what you see.

  • An error code or message: note the exact text or number. Photograph the screen.
  • Red or blinking light: note the pattern, and which light.
  • No display at all: the inverter may have no supply, or it may have failed.
  • “Grid fault”, “no grid” or similar: your grid supply may be off or unstable.

Do not repeatedly switch the inverter on and off to see if it “resets”. One normal restart after checking the supply is fine, but repeated cycling can hide the real fault.

Step 3: Check the power supply and breakers

  • Is there grid power at home? A normal grid-tied solar inverter switches itself off when the grid fails, as a safety feature, and restarts when the grid returns. This is normal, and is why a rooftop system without a battery does not run during a power cut.
  • Look at the breakers (MCBs) in the solar AC box. If one is down (tripped), do not keep forcing it back on. A breaker that trips again points to a real problem.
  • Look for a monitoring-only fault. If only the app or WiFi stick shows “offline”, the system may be generating fine and only the monitoring has lost its connection. Compare with the number on the inverter or your electricity meter.

What to note before you book a repair

  • The exact error text, code or light pattern, with a photo if you can.
  • When it started, and whether it happens all day or only at some hours.
  • Any recent event: storm, power surge, construction, new shade, or a recent cleaning.
  • Your system size (kW) and inverter brand, if you know them.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my solar system generating less than before?

Common reasons are dust or bird droppings on the panels, new shade, cloudy or foggy weather, a tripped breaker, an inverter fault or a loose connection. Start with the simple checks: clean panels, shade, weather and any error on the inverter.

Why does my inverter stop when the power goes out?

A normal grid-tied solar inverter switches off when grid power fails, as a safety feature. It restarts when the grid returns. Only systems with a battery or a hybrid inverter can run during an outage.

Is it safe to open the inverter or touch the panel wiring?

No. Solar panels keep producing DC power whenever light falls on them, even with the inverter off. Do not open the inverter or handle wiring and connectors. Call a technician.
